<h3 id="description">Description</h3>
<p>A general historical survey of the cultural and technological
developments in sound recording, music and sound for the cinema, radio
and television. Avant-garde composition, electronic, digital and
interactive music, and the recent influence of the internet on sound and
culture will also be discussed.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>“If we do not begin our discussions of the future of music by
considering what music is for—and by extension, what culture is for—the
answers will be decided for us.” – Jonathan Sterne, <em>MP3: The
Cultural Meaning of a Format</em><a href="#fn1" class="footnote-ref"
id="fnref1" role="doc-noteref"><sup>1</sup></a></p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="learning-objectives">Learning objectives</h3>
<p>By the end of this course, you should be able to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Identify key figures, periods, and movements in the history of sound
media.</li>
<li>Apply a critical framework regarding racial, gender-related,
socioeconomic, environmental and ability justice toward historical and
present-day sound media.</li>
<li>Form and express your own opinions about the influence of
technological, social, and other factors in steering sonic culture.</li>
<li>Explain what “sound studies” means as an academic field and how it
expands approaches to audio media.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="course-structure">Course structure</h3>
<p>Our course is organized into five modules: <em>culture
vs. technology</em>, <em>the radio-record industry and its wake</em>,
<em>sound and image</em>, <em>labor and automation</em>, and <em>sound
affects</em>. In a typical week, our Monday session will be
discussion-centered while our Wednesday session will center on a lecture
component.</p>
<h3 id="quizzes">Quizzes</h3>
<p>Each of the five modules will culminate with an in-class quiz. These
will vary in format, but you should expect to do some short-answer
writing in response to a few questions that correspond to our learning
objectives in the course.</p>
<h3 id="voice-check-ins-for-readinglistening">Voice check-ins for
reading/listening</h3>
<p>For each reading (or podcast, etc.) I introduce on a Wednesday for
you to complete before Monday’s class, you will leave a <em>voice
check-in</em> about it by <strong>Friday at 5pm</strong>. We will use a
Teams chat channel to gather these check-ins, which will be available to
your classmates. Check-ins are specifically <em>not</em> reading
“responses.” I will not expect you to have completed the entire reading
before recording your check-in (though I will expect you to complete
each reading by the time of our next class session), and I
<em>definitely</em> do not expect (nor want) you to <em>summarize</em>
the reading. What I want to know is: how’s it going with this reading?
What’s tripping you up? What are you excited to talk about when we cover
this in class?</p>
<p>I will grade voice check-ins pass/fail according to two criteria: 1)
the check-in arrives before the deadline so others can benefit from
hearing it, and 2) the check-in contributes something substantive toward
our efforts to draw insights out of the reading/listening.</p>
<p><em>The main goal of this recurring assignment is to make your
reading easier and more interesting—if you approach it in that spirit
and let me know what’s working or not working, I’m confident we can meet
that goal together.</em></p>
<h3 id="assessment-and-attendance">Assessment and attendance</h3>
<p>Your grade for this course will be determined 30% by class
attendance, 30% by your weekly reading check-ins, 30% by the 5 quizzes,
and 10% by an informal “show and tell” presentation to the class during
our last two sessions.</p>
